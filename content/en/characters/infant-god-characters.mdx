## The Nature of Divinity: Crowns and Ascension

In many mythical realms, godhood is not strictly an inherent genetic trait; it is often tied to physical vessels of power known as Crowns. In the Lands of the Old Faith, the original First Gods shaped the terrain using divine implements like axes and hammers, eventually leaving behind powerful artifacts before severing their realm from mortal lands.

A mortal creature can transform into an ascending divinity by claiming one of these divine relics. Conversely, if an entity is stripped of its artifact, their godhood vanishes, reducing them back to mortal status or leaving them entirely powerless.

## Key Infant God Characters and Nascent Divinities

When analyzing **Infant God characters** and fledgling deities, we look at beings that represent nascent godhood, newly ascended cult leaders, or young entities born directly into divine lineages. 

### The Chosen Child (God of Gold)

The Chosen Child represents one of the purest examples of nascent divine entities. Unlike the Heretic Gods who rely on Crowns stolen or scavenged after the ancient purges, the Chosen Child belongs to the celestial lineage of the Gods of Gold. Community reports and environmental lore point to this young deity possessing innate angelic qualities, untainted by the bloody artifact conflicts that defined the mortal planes.

### The Lamb: From Sacrifice to Supreme Entity

The journey of The Lamb perfectly illustrates how **Infant God characters** develop through strategic cult leadership. Saved from execution by Narinder (The One Who Waits) and bestowed with the Red Crown, The Lamb begins as a fledgling godling. 

Through accumulating devotion, defeating ancient Bishops, and managing followers, The Lamb evolves from a mere vessel into a paramount divine ruler. ## The Fall of the Old Pantheon and the Divine Purge

Before the rise of contemporary deities, hundreds of gods inhabited the realm. Understanding the historical purge explains why so few complete deities exist today and why newly emerging **Infant God characters** encounter a landscape filled with ruins and bone-strewn temples.

The collapse of the old order occurred in three distinct historical phases:

1. **The Madness of Chemach:** The distributor of divine crowns lost sanity, scattering powerful relics across mortal realms.
2. **The Rise of Heretic Warlords:** Entities discovered these lost crowns, claiming artificial godhood without natural right.
3. **The Bishop Purge:** A ruthless alliance (Leshy, Heket, Kallamar, Shamura, and Narinder) initiated a systematic extermination of rival cults, stealing followers and destroying temples to eliminate competition.

| Historical Phase | Catalyzing Event | Outcome on Divine Hierarchy | Impact on Fledgling Gods |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primordial Era** | Departure of the First Gods | Creation of physical boundaries via the Oath of the Crown | Left behind dormant artifacts and crystallised tears |
| **The Great Scatter** | Chemach's Madness | Hundreds of Crowns lost throughout the wilderness | Allowed random mortals to elevate into nascent deities |
| **The Great Purge** | Bishop Coalition Warfare | Near-total extinction of rival pantheons | Confined worship strictly to four cardinal Bishops |
| **The Red Dawn** | Execution of the Last Lamb | The rise of a new divine champion | Total collapse of the Old Faith hierarchy |

## Managing Cult Devotion for Nascent Deities

In player experience and community gameplay strategies, the survival of fledgling divine figures depends directly on how efficiently they manage their cult compounds. Nascent gods do not have infinite reserves of innate power; their strength waxes and wanes based on follower loyalty, ritual execution, and base maintenance.

### Key Operational Priorities for Ascending Leaders

- **Establish Consistent Prayer Cycles:** Build shrines early to capture continuous devotion from followers.
- **Maintain Faith Hygiene:** Prevent disease, hunger, and dissent, which directly weaken the divine resonance of the Crown.
- **Acquire God Tears:** Venture into Purgatory realms to collect fragments of primordial tears for high-tier upgrades.
- **Neutralize Heretic Relics:** Ensure rival cult relics are purified or utilized to strengthen your cult's offensive doctrines.

| Cult Component | Optimal Setup | Divine Benefit | Common Pitfall to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Temple Architecture** | Stone/Gold Cathedral | Maximum Faith generation per sermon | Neglecting daily sermons during crusades |
| **Follower Cabal** | 15–20 High-Loyalty Devotees | Constant stream of Devotion currency | Overcrowding leading to illness outbreaks |
| **Sacrificial Altar** | Central compound placement | Instant resurrection or power boosts | Over-sacrificing, which tanks cult morale |

By understanding the delicate balance between crown artifacts, follower faith, and historical purges, players can guide their nascent **Infant God characters** from vulnerable figureheads into the undisputed rulers of the realm.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What defines Infant God characters in dark fantasy lore?
**Infant God characters** are young, newly ascended, or nascent deities who have either inherited natural celestial divinity (such as the Chosen Child) or claimed an artifact like a Crown to transcend mortality. They must gather devotion and establish a cult following to mature their powers.

### Can an ascended deity lose their godhood permanently?
Yes. If an ascended deity's Crown is removed or destroyed, or if their entire follower base is eliminated or stripped of faith by weapons like the Gauntlets of Marchosias, the deity loses their divine standing and reverts to mortal form.

### How do God Tears influence newly emerging gods?
God Tears are crystallized remnants shed by the First Gods. Ascended characters can gather these fragments from beasts or purgatorial realms to trade with mystic vendors for powerful blessings, doctrine stones, and celestial upgrades.
